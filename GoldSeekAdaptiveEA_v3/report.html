Critical

§ 03 · P0
P0-1

Positions are opened with no stop, then protected afterwards

line 1473

OrderSend is called with SL and TP hardcoded to 0.0. Protection arrives only later, through ApplyInputExits → ModifyActiveStops. Between the fill and the first successful OrderModify the position carries no broker-side stop at all.

The retry path widens the window: each failure waits 2 seconds, and the fail-safe market exit fires only after 5 attempts — roughly 10 seconds of unprotected exposure, followed by a close that itself retries on a backoff and needs the same trade context that was already failing. The fail-safe is also gated on StopLoss_PriceUSD > 0.0, so with a zero stop there is no escalation whatsoever.

Failure scenario The EA fills a buy one second before a CPI print. The broker rejects OrderModify through the spread blowout. Ten seconds later gold has moved $12 against the position, and only then does the fail-safe attempt a market close — at whatever price an unlimited-slippage close returns. Configured risk was $20; realised risk is unbounded.

Fix — attempt stops inside OrderSend, fall back to the modify path only when the broker rejects stops at open, and remove the zero-stop gate on the fail-safe.

P0-2

No daily loss limit — and the counter that exists is never enforced

line 823

EntriesToday() is substantial machinery: it persists a day key and count, reconciles against the terminal's open orders and full history every 5 seconds, and defends against under-counting after a crash. It is read in four places — telemetry, a log line, the panel, and a warm-up call.

It is never compared to a limit. The panel renders the count followed by the literal string " (UNCAPPED)". There is no daily loss cap, no consecutive-loss cooldown, no maximum trades per day, and no equity floor anywhere in the file.

This compounds with re-entry: ResetForFreshCycle re-arms the state machine on close, and OnTick can open the next position on the very same tick.

Failure scenario Gold chops in a $25 band while M5 structure and ATR expansion both read as qualified. A trade stops out at $20. Structure has not changed, so the same direction re-qualifies and the EA re-enters. Repeat. Nothing in the code stops this running all session.

Fix — enforce a daily realised-loss cap and a post-loss cooldown in AcquisitionEvidenceReady. The counter and persistence layer already exist; only the comparison is missing.

P0-3

Slippage is effectively unlimited, on entry as well as exit

line 90

NO_EA_SLIPPAGE_CAP_POINTS = 1000000 is passed to both OrderSend and OrderClose. On 2-digit gold that is a $10,000 tolerance. There is no maximum-spread veto anywhere — the panel displays the spread, but nothing acts on it.

This is deliberate; OnInit announces "no EA spread/slippage veto; actual execution costs remain auditable." Auditable after the fact is not the same as bounded. Note the asymmetry: unlimited tolerance on the exit is defensible because it guarantees the position closes. On the entry it is not — a rejected entry costs nothing.

Fix — keep it on OrderClose, cap it on OrderSend, and add a maximum-spread precondition to the entry.

P0-4

No instrument guard, on a model defined entirely in absolute USD

lines 102–109

Every threshold is an absolute price quantity, not a normalised one: a $20 stop, $7.50 minimum remaining travel, a $30 calibration target, $15 adverse, and adverse clamps of $5–15 and $8–20. There is no check anywhere that the chart symbol is gold — no symbol test, no Digits test, no contract-size test. OnInit validates lot sizing and nothing else.

Failure scenario Attached to EURUSD, MIN_REMAINING_TRAVEL_USD = 7.50 means 7.50 price units — 75,000 pips — against an expected-travel value clamped to [6, 60]. The gate can never be satisfied and the EA silently never trades. On an index CFD the same constants are far too tight instead. Either way the panel just reads SCANNING FOR A DEVELOPING MOVE forever.

Fix — validate contract size and tick value in OnInit, or use an explicit symbol whitelist, and refuse to load otherwise.

High

§ 04 · P1
P1-1

The calibration writes to a different bin than it reads from

lines 587, 1503, 1533

A concrete logic error, not a modelling opinion. The read side bins on the offline score: CalibrationBin(offlineProbability). The write side records the outcome into the bin of the already-calibrated output: CalibrationBin(decisionP30).

Because calibration blends the offline score toward a lower posterior, the output frequently lands in a lower bin than the input. When it does, the bin the EA reads from stops receiving outcomes — permanently. Simulated with a steady offline score of 0.150 and every trade labelled MISS: 

trade   1: readbin=1 writebin=1  calP30=0.1500  probQuality=0.846  (n_read=1)
trade  20: readbin=1 writebin=1  calP30=0.1204  probQuality=0.618  (n_read=20)
trade  50: readbin=1 writebin=0  calP30=0.1183  probQuality=0.603  (n_read=20)
trade 200: readbin=1 writebin=0  calP30=0.1183  probQuality=0.603  (n_read=20)

final counters per bin: [180, 20, 0, 0, 0]

Bin 1 freezes at 20 observations. The remaining 180 outcomes pile into bin 0, which these setups never read. calP30 is pinned at 0.1183 forever — the online calibration has silently stopped calibrating.

Fix — bin on the offline score on both sides. One line.

P1-2

Calibration counters never decay, so the entry gate tightens for life

line 596

Nothing ever decrements, ages, or caps the hit/miss counters. They live in terminal global variables keyed by account and symbol, surviving restarts, recompiles and input changes indefinitely, while learnedWeight only ever grows. Where read and write bins do agree, the result is monotone decay rather than a freeze — probQuality falls from 0.538 at trade 1 to 0.025 by trade 200.

That term carries weight 0.12 in opportunityScore, and entry requires 0.30. The floor guaranteed by the other gates is only about 0.215:

TermGuaranteed at the entry gateContribution
structuralQuality(0.7629 − 0.62) / 1.60 = 0.0890.025
motionQuality1.0 — required by M5MotionAligned0.140
remainingQuality7.50 / 30 = 0.250.035
exhaustion headroom1 − 0.78 = 0.220.015
Floorvs. a required 0.300.215

So roughly 0.085 must come from the soft terms, of which the decaying probability term can supply up to 0.12. The entry gate silently tightens over the EA's lifetime, with no user-visible signal and no way to reset short of deleting global variables by hand.

Fix — add a decay factor or rolling window, and expose a reset.

P1-3

The calibration label is unreachable by construction

lines 1818–1823

Success is a $30 favourable excursion. But the default stop is $20, and the model's own adverse exit triggers at $8–20 against. Failure, meanwhile, is recorded on almost any terminating condition: MAE ≥ $15, or any model exit reason at all, or age beyond the horizon.

The label is therefore biased toward MISS regardless of whether the signal had merit — which is what drives both findings above. The learner is being fed a target its own risk management prevents it from hitting.

Fix — measure against a target the trade management can reach, or record censored outcomes explicitly instead of folding them into MISS.

P1-4

Editing any input while a trade is open silently discards user-set stops

lines 1227–1234

OnDeinit with REASON_PARAMETERS writes a reconfigure flag. On re-init, RecoverActiveTrade clears both manual-override flags, forces an exit sync, and recomputes stops from the inputs — overwriting whatever is on the order.

Failure scenario A trade is $14 in profit. The user drags the stop to breakeven by hand, then opens the Inputs tab to change LotSize for the next trade. On OK, the EA resets the stop to entry − $20. A risk-free trade is risking $20 again, with no warning and no log line naming the discarded level.

Fix — preserve manual overrides across a parameter change, or warn explicitly before overwriting.

Medium and low

§ 05 · P2 · P3
P2-1

The dual-direction tracker fallback is dead code

lines 707–715, 1363

BestTrackerDirection() returns the structural direction when it qualifies, and otherwise falls through to an opportunity-score comparison. That fallback can never produce a trade: its only consumer immediately calls M5StructuralReady, whose first line is if(direction != g_market.structuralDirection) return(false). If the fallback returns the structural direction, that check already failed one line earlier; if it returns the opposite, the guard rejects it.

Search state can only ever advance on a single structural sign test. The trackers still matter as an entry filter — but at the point of choosing a side, the "dual-direction pursuit engine" is not dual.

  • P2-2 · Any foreign position blocks the EA silently. The block check ignores magic numbers entirely; the adoption check matches only the current magic. A manual hedge, another EA, or a leftover v1/v2 position freezes this EA indefinitely while the panel still reads SCANNING. The three magic-number paths disagree with each other: legacy magics are recognised for counting only, adoption uses the current magic, blocking uses none.
  • P2-3 · Two instances on one symbol corrupt each other. The persistence key is account + symbol + suffix — no chart ID, no timeframe. Two XAUUSD charts share one namespace for ticket, stops, peak and calibration state, and both match the same magic.
  • P2-4 · OnInit hard-fails when broker symbol data isn't loaded yet. MarketInfo legitimately returns 0 at terminal startup and over the weekend; the EA converts that into INIT_PARAMETERS_INCORRECT and never retries. It is dead until manually re-attached — a real availability bug for an EA meant to run unattended.
  • P2-5 · A zero stop is accepted without warning. With both stop and take-profit at zero the position has no broker-side exit of any kind, and the protection fail-safe is itself gated on a positive stop.
  • P2-6 · A full order-history scan every 5 seconds, for a display-only value. Driven by the 250 ms panel refresh. Worse in the strategy tester, where history grows monotonically through the run.
  • P3 · Dead weight (7 items). Six of eight DestinationProbability evaluations per bar are never read; probabilityUp/Down are write-only; both advertised BENCHMARK_* constants are unused; g_freshM1Bars and g_resetTime exist only to feed each other; ReturnToSearch ignores its parameter; the M1 sigma is computed twice per bar; and mode probabilities survive session gaps that reset everything else.

What the code gets right

§ 06

Worth stating plainly, because it is the part worth keeping.

  • No repainting. Every indicator and price read uses shift ≥ 1. Backtest results will not be inflated by look-ahead — the single most common defect in EAs of this type, and a claim most competitors cannot make.
  • The barrier mathematics is correct. The two-sided first-passage formula and the reflection-principle hit-by-horizon formula both match the standard results, with consistent per-minute units, and the Abramowitz–Stegun CDF handles the negative tail properly.
  • The regime transition prior is valid. 0.72·pᵢ + 0.28·(1−pᵢ)/4 is a proper row-stochastic mix: 0.72 self-persistence, 0.07 to each alternative.
  • Division-by-zero discipline is consistent. ATR and sigma floored at Point, path-length guards, guarded tick size — no unguarded divide anywhere in the file.
  • Tick-count wraparound is handled correctly via unsigned arithmetic, a detail most MQL4 code gets wrong.
  • Crash and restart recovery is thorough. Persisted state is validated against the live ticket before being trusted, mode probabilities are renormalised on restore, and the entry counter explicitly defends against a crash between fill and increment.
  • Session-gap handling is correct. Both gap branches refuse to inject the cross-gap price jump into CUSUM or thesis evidence — a subtle failure mode that is easy to miss.

What the product actually is

§ 07 · Product

An EMA20/EMA30 ribbon trend filter on M5, gated by ATR expansion, requiring three-way agreement between ribbon slope, price-to-EMA gap and 3-bar velocity — entering with a fixed $20 stop, no take-profit, and exiting on an evidence-decay rule.

That is a legitimate, recognisable strategy. It is not what the file presents itself as. Everything that decides direction flows through one equal-weight average of four winsorized z-scores. Everything else is a modifier, or inert.

ComponentPresented asActual influence on the decision
Barrier probability, first-passage, normal CDF Core probability engine ≤ 3.7% of the entry threshold
5-mode regime classifier Regime awareness Real but modest — damping and score adjustments
Dual-direction tracker scoring "Dual-direction pursuit engine" Dead for direction selection (P2-1)
Online Bayesian calibration Learns from live outcomes Provably stops learning (P1-1)
CUSUM change detection Regime break detection Small — 0.05 coefficient in the logit
Quantified

The sophistication is decorative

The EA computes eight destination probabilities every M1 bar, each running two first-passage formulas, a normal CDF and several exponentials. Six are never read. The two that are enter the decision through a single coefficient of 0.012:

StepMaximum influence
Contribution to the offline score≤ 0.012
→ probabilityQuality≤ 0.092
→ opportunityScore (weight 0.12)≤ 0.011
→ share of the 0.30 entry gate≤ 3.7%

The entire stochastic-process layer can move the entry decision by under four percent of the threshold it feeds. A customer paying for "causal probability modelling" is paying for a rounding term.

  • An overfitting signal in plain sight. STRUCTURAL_SCORE_THRESHOLD = 0.7629589434 — ten significant figures on a threshold applied to a four-term average. On the mean of four unit-variance inputs that sits between 0.76σ (perfectly correlated) and 1.53σ (independent); since EMA gap, ribbon velocity and 3-bar velocity all measure the same move, the truth is nearer the correlated end. It is roughly a one-sigma trend filter, dressed as a calibrated threshold.
  • No learned weights exist. The "frozen train-only standardization" is a plain unweighted mean. The only fitted artifacts in the entire file are eight winsorization bounds, four means and four standard deviations. The code references a holdout — that evidence does not ship with the product, so none of it can be claimed.
  • The advertised benchmark is not implemented. Both benchmark constants appear nowhere else in the file. The panel's "BENCHMARK $50 LEG | $15 REVERSAL" is a hardcoded display string; no code path targets a $50 leg or enforces a $15 reversal.
  • The entry bar sits well below the risk taken. The EA will accept a trade whose own model says $7.50 of travel remains, while risking $20 — roughly 2.7:1 against, requiring a ~73% hit rate merely to break even before costs.
  • Credit where due: the exit rules protect the right tail. With no take-profit, the payoff depends on letting winners run, and the exits are built for that — the stale-horizon exit only fires when MFE is under $15, and failure-to-launch needs three invalidation bars plus weak evidence. Winners are not cut short by design. The problem is that unlimited entry slippage and no spread filter attack exactly the thin edge this payoff profile depends on.
  • Expect it to get quieter, then expect a support ticket. Entry needs roughly eight conditions to hold at once, and the calibration ratchet tightens the gate further over time — invisibly from the panel, and resettable only by hand-deleting terminal global variables.

Claims

§ 08

Defensible today

  • MT4 EA for XAUUSD, M5 structure with M1 tracking, one position at a time
  • Trend-following entries on an EMA20/30 ribbon with ATR-expansion confirmation
  • Fixed-dollar stop, optional profit lock and trailing, user-owned exit settings
  • Survives terminal restarts and reconciles its state against the broker
  • Uses only closed bars — no repainting, and verifiable in the source
  • Writes a per-trade telemetry CSV for independent auditing

Not defensible

  • Any win rate, drawdown, profit factor or return figure
  • "Adaptive" or "learns from live outcomes" — the loop is broken
  • "$50 leg / $15 reversal benchmark" — not implemented
  • "Probability-calibrated" — the layer moves the decision by under 4%
  • "Dual-direction pursuit engine" — it is a single sign test
  • Anything implying capped or known risk per day

Path to a sellable product

Make it safe

All four P0 items: send stops with the order, add a daily loss cap and post-loss cooldown, cap entry slippage and gate on spread, guard the instrument. None of this touches the model.

Make it honest

Fix the bin mismatch, give the counters decay and a reset, redefine the calibration label, and either wire in or delete the inert probability layer. Then rewrite the description to match the code. "EMA ribbon + ATR expansion with evidence-based exits" is an easier product to sell honestly than a "causal probability engine" a technical customer can disprove in twenty minutes.

Make it provable — this is the real gate

Tick-data backtest at 99% modelling quality with realistic spread and commission across ≥ 3 years including the 2020 and 2022 gold regimes — with unlimited slippage in the code, anything less is meaningless. Then walk-forward validation that re-derives the standardization constants and the structural threshold per window rather than reusing 0.7629589434. Then 90 days minimum of demo forward-testing on the intended broker, using the telemetry CSV as the record; given the low expected trade frequency, a shorter window will not produce a meaningful sample. Publish trade count, win rate, average win/loss, max drawdown and profit factor against the exact build they came from.

Fix the four safety items, repair the learning loop, describe the strategy as what it is — then forward-test it before any of it is sold.
