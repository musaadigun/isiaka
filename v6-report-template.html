Verdict

V6 is a different system and a materially better one. Every blocking finding from the V4 audit is fixed, and fixed correctly. The logistic model is gone, replaced by a deterministic three-timeframe threshold stack. The result is consistent with a small real edge — and not yet established as one.

The structural win: dropping the trailing stop collapsed the intrabar-ordering ambiguity that made V4 unmeasurable. Both orderings now give exactly the same number. V4's ±$390 bracket, the thing that left its sign undetermined, does not exist in V6.

One question I can't answer and you can: what data were the 17 thresholds chosen on? If they were tuned on this gold history, everything below is in-sample. If they were set a priori, the out-of-window agreement in §4 is real evidence. This changes the interpretation more than any further computation would.

The edge is the 120-minute horizon

90 of 101 exits are the time stop; only 11 are stop losses. Adding a $30 take profit and removing the time stop collapses the result to −$15 (PF 0.988). So the strategy is: qualified trigger → hold about two hours → exit at market. The $25 stop participates in roughly one trade in nine.

That is a legitimate way to build a system, but it means the risk management is doing almost nothing, and the entire edge rides on the one parameter the sweep above shows is fitted.

Audit — new findings

V6-1

The staleness guard was removed

V4 rejected any regime bar older than 12 hours. V6's GVLastFullyClosedShift (196–208) checks only that the bar has closed — there is no upper bound on age.

Measured on your data: H1 regime bars up to 54 hours old, 530 valid bars affected, and 6 qualified signals fired on H1 data more than 12 hours stale. After a weekend or a feed outage the EA trades Monday off Friday's H1 regime. P/L impact is small — restoring a 12-hour bound gives +$461.88 against +$446.27 — but that is a six-signal sample, not a reason to leave it out.

if(decisionTime-barClose > maxAgeSeconds) return(-1);   // 3*tf*60 for M5, 12*3600 for H1
V6-2

atrM5Slow is dead but still gates every signal

Line 225 computes GVATR(M5,48,closedM5) and line 228 rejects the whole calculation if it is ≤ 0. The value is never used again — in V4 it fed the volatilityRatio feature, which no longer exists. Since GVATR returns 0 if any of 48 true ranges is zero, a single flat M5 bar silently kills the signal for no reason. Delete it, or use it.

V6-3

SendEntry returns false after a successful open

Two paths (1063–1069, 1084–1092) return false when the order is already open, and g_lastEntryTime is never reached. Nothing breaks — the cache is invalidated at 1061 and FindManagedTicket() blocks re-entry — but a caller cannot distinguish "no position" from "position open, protection pending".

V6-4

MaximumM1ChaseATR is measured in M5 ATR

Line 314 divides by atrM5, not atrM1. Someone setting "1 ATR of chase" gets roughly 5× more tolerance than they expect. The behaviour is defensible; the name is not.

V6-5

GV_STATE_EXHAUSTING is never assigned

Defined at 24, rendered at 129, tested at 1317 and 1468 — but no code path sets it. Half the velocity-exit condition is dead, so UseVelocityExit is weaker than it reads.

V6-6

Two sources of truth for bar requirements

GV_REQUIRED_M5_BARS 80 and GV_REQUIRED_H1_BARS 40 are checked at 213–214, then GVLastFullyClosedShift is called with hard-coded 79 and 30 at 220–221. Four numbers, two of them silently authoritative. (V6-7: result.velocityM15 holds an M1 value — leftover field name.)

What to do next

  1. Answer the provenance question. Were the thresholds and the SL/hold defaults chosen on this gold history? That determines whether §4 is validation or in-sample fitting, and it moves the conclusion further than any further computation would.
  2. Get more M1 data. 47 trading days cannot settle this. Two years of M1 would let the full stack be tested properly. Nothing substitutes for it.
  3. Re-pick the hold time on the long sample, or stop treating it as a constant. 90–150 all work on the 560-trade core; choose from that range on evidence, or make it scale with ATR.
  4. Fix V6-1 and V6-2 before live. Neither loses money today; both are correctness issues that will bite on a Monday or a bad feed.
  5. Do not re-enable trailing without re-measuring. Its absence is why this backtest has a determinate answer at all — and on this sample it cut net from $446 to $312 anyway.
